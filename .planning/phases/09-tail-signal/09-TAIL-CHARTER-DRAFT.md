## Header

- **Signal** Tail — the priced asymmetry of the risk-neutral density. **Clock** daily (fast), same as volatility. **Ledger status: none** — volatility carries none (context axis), stock-bond correlation carries one because sign-zero is a real mechanical boundary; index skew has been persistently negative since 1987, so any threshold here is an arbitrary percentile knob, the exact failure the volatility reframe removed.
- **Assumption monitored** — **"downside protection is priced normally."** *Reframed* from the kickoff's "the return distribution is its normal shape," a physical claim this data cannot measure.
- **Maturity ceiling `research`, registered before the look** so a strong result cannot argue it away: (i) international replication is **impossible, not merely unattempted**; (ii) vendor-computed index, not reconstructible from free data; (iii) pre-2011 history is retrospective backfill; (iv) Cboe has announced a recalculation, so the series is a moving object.
- **Investor question** — *"How expensive is protection against a large equity decline right now, relative to at-the-money, and how unusual is that price versus its own history?"*

## What the signal IS — risk-neutral, not physical (governs everything)

`q(x) = p(x)·m(x)/E^P[m]`: a strike cross-section identifies only the risk-neutral density `q`, never its split into physical density `p` and pricing kernel `m` (Aït-Sahalia & Lo 2000; Jackwerth 2000). Ross (2015) recovery needs transition independence, which Borovička–Hansen–Scheinkman (2016) show assumes away the permanent SDF component; Jackwerth & Menner (2020) find recovered densities incompatible with subsequent returns. Bollerslev & Todorov (2011, *JF*): Q left-jump intensity exceeds P by 275–430×, and **the left/right asymmetry skew measures is significant only under Q** — the physical asymmetry is not distinguishable from zero.
**So the signal measures the PRICE of one month of downside protection relative to at-the-money. A price, never a probability.** If the physical claim is required instead, the honest outcome is **DROP** — no free in-scope data can measure it.

## Stage-0 gate (re-run, default NO) and mechanism

**PASS — conditional, at reduced confidence.** G1/G2: the market's own quoted compensation for bearing large-decline risk. G3 unique on two axes — **measure** (every admitted signal is physical P; this is the only risk-neutral Q reading) and **moment** (volatility is width, this is asymmetry). G4: "calm with cheap protection" vs "calm with historically dear protection" is separable by no admitted signal. G5 **splits, and the split is the point**: strong prior on the **object** (Rubinstein 1994 crash-o-phobia; Bakshi–Kapadia–Madan 2003; Bates 2008; Bollerslev–Todorov 2011), weak prior on the **instrument** — Cao–Ruan–Zhang (2020) call SKEW "very noisy"; Bevilacqua & Tunaru (2021) find only the put-only variant carries signal. Sound mechanism, poor instrument: the mechanism gate is binary and passes, measurement validity is a separate non-compensatory axis and grades **L**. *Kickoff correction:* Kelly–Jiang (2014) is a **physical** Hill-estimator tail beta — different object, different measure; it does not support an options-implied signal. Retired.
**Mechanism — risk-premium channel: the jump/tail premium plus intermediary supply.** Insuring a large index decline is undiversifiable and unhedgeable by trading the index (a jump cannot be replicated by continuous trading), so it must be compensated: Bollerslev–Todorov (2011) attribute a median 5.2% of the equity premium and ~60% of the variance premium to the left tail; Carr & Wu (2009) establish the variance premium as separately priced; Bates (2008) models crash-risk equilibrium; demand-based pricing adds constrained dealer capacity. **It is a price of risk, not a mispricing** — publishing "protection is at the 95th percentile of expensiveness" makes nobody supply it more cheaply; the price *is* the signal. Distinctness from volatility: Andersen–Fusari–Todorov (2017) find "a sharp separation between the dynamics of the actual jump risk and its pricing"; Bollerslev–Todorov–Xu (2015) find adding VIX² to the left-jump measure "hardly increases" its content.
**Counter-evidence, registered because it cuts against us.** Kozhan–Neuberger–Schneider (2013, *RFS*) find skew risk "tightly related to variance risk," with an insignificant premium once variance is hedged out — a real prior that V4 fires. And risk-neutral skewness is the third cumulant over variance^(3/2), so SKEW and VIX are two nonlinear functions of one state vector, which is why their reported relation is unstable in sign (−0.23 in levels, Bevilacqua & Tunaru 2021; positive in daily changes, Liu & Faff 2017). Hence V4 tests the **residual**, not a correlation threshold.

## Validation bars → reject conditions (all run ONCE, one frozen script, after sign-off)

| bar | test | reject |
|---|---|---|
| **V1** definitional fidelity | `SKEW = 100 − 10·S`, S = risk-neutral skewness of the 30d SPX log return; the BKM (2003) lineage is an explicit Cboe citation. Signal reports `S = (100 − SKEW)/10` in native sign, not the 100-scale, which inverts the sign and reads as "up = safer" | **R1** the methodology stops supporting the mapping. Known future trigger: the announced 25-delta put/call replacement is a *different object* — re-charter, do not patch |
| **V2** causal / point-in-time | `assert_causal(build, panel)` passes; rarity is causal expanding-window, never full-sample rank. Dual read: full history vs live-publication era side by side (pre-2011 is backfill — inputs unrevised, but not point-in-time *available*) | **R2** guard fails, or the two rarity bases differ in a way that would mislead ⇒ truncate to the live era, disclosed |
| **V3** methodology homogeneity | no distributional break in `S` at the registered dates: 1990-01-02 (backfill start), the 2011 launch, 2014-10-06 (SPX Weeklys added to VIX-family interpolation — evidence says VIX, not SKEW; cheap to confirm) | **R3** material break ⇒ truncate to the homogeneous segment; if too short for an honest rarity read, **drop** |
| **V4** **redundancy with volatility — decisive** | (a) PRIMARY: causal expanding-window regression of `S` on the volatility level **and its log** (two-term form fixed here, no later specification search) — is the residual stable, interpretable, non-negligible? (b) do divergence episodes form an interpretable class? (c) rank correlation in levels **and** changes — diagnostic only, **no threshold, no reject wired to it** | **R4 KILL** when (a) the residual is thin/unstable **AND** (b) the episodes are unstructured. If the residual survives but the raw level does not, the residual becomes the presented reading and the level a diagnostic (the EBP precedent). **Registered prior: given Kozhan et al., R4 firing is likely, not a surprise** — a null is a clean output, never rescued by a specification search |
| **V5** sub-period replication | rarity calibration, persistence order-of-magnitude and asymmetry sign hold in the backfill era and the live era *separately*. Explicitly **weaker** than the Japan/Europe bar; caps evidence maturity, never lifts it | **R5** properties hold in the backfill era but not the live era ⇒ a property of the vendor's retrospective algorithm, not of the market. **Kill** |
| **V6** out-of-hypothesis-sample | **resolved in advance as a documented failure** — no risk-neutral-skewness index exists for Japan or Europe at any tier (see data limit 4) | **R6 not a kill; a pre-committed cap.** Evidence maturity **L**; maturity tag held at `research` permanently unless purchased data is later obtained |
| **V7** confound-check | any "environments with similarly-priced protection looked like X" must condition on the volatility reading and say so | **R7** an unconfoundable context statistic **is not reported at all** — kills the statistic, not the signal |
| **V8** rarity is not a measurement artifact | dispersion of `S` must not decline monotonically with the index level; the rarity read must be materially unchanged under the restricted start | **R8** kills the **long sample**, not the signal ⇒ truncate (2000-01 built; 2011 the conservative fallback) |

**Registered design constraint:** no forward-return, drawdown, hit-rate, or lead/lag statistic is computed anywhere in this phase — not as a headline, not as a robustness check, not in a scratch script. The signal makes no forward claim, so there is nothing for such a statistic to validate, and computing one is how a measurement system drifts into a prediction system.

## Data limits (verified 2026-08-06 against `data/processed/tail_daily.csv`)

Coverage: `skew` 1990-01-02…2026-08-04 (9198 non-null) · `vix` 1990-01-02… · `vix3m`/`term_slope` **2009-09-18** · `vix9d` **2011-01-04**. File 9248 rows; MANIFEST entry present.
1. **`term_slope` DROPPED** *(built as dropped)*: starts 2009 (one stress episode); `VIX3M/VIX − 1` is the *same moment at two horizons* with zero asymmetry information, and `REGIME-SENSOR-ARCHITECTURE.md` already rejects "VIX/VRP as a separate axis (collapses onto vol)"; and the curve mixes three option universes (VIX9D/VIX end-of-week SPXW, VIX3M end-of-month SPXW, VIX6M AM-settled SPX), so part of any slope is a contract-set artifact. *Honest tension, recorded:* a VSTOXX term-slope signal **could** be replicated internationally while risk-neutral skewness cannot — the replicable component is exactly the one that duplicates volatility. Choosing it would be designing around available data (D-10). Keep the mechanism, take the cap.
2. **Start restricted to 2000-01-01** *(built)*: Aschakulporn & Zhang (2022) require a strike step ≤0.1% of forward for risk-neutral *skewness* error <1e-3. SPX strikes are quoted in dollars, so the constraint eases mechanically as the index rises — 1990 (~350, $5 step) violates it ~14×, mid-1990s ~8×, today 0.083% satisfies it. The error is **not stationary; it shrinks monotonically with the index level**, so a long sample manufactures a spurious trend in the expanding rarity descriptor, which is the core of what this signal reports. Deep-OTM listings were also sparser early, and a third moment weights exactly those strikes most. Root citation: Jiang & Tian (2005) for model-free implied *variance*.
3. **The published history has ALREADY been silently revised — the most serious finding.** Cboe's 2011 documentation gives an all-time low/high of **101.09 (1991-03-21) / 146.88 (1998-10-16)**; today's file gives **101.31 / 146.22** on those same dates — values moved by 0.22 and 0.66 index points, far beyond rounding — and the file's own minimum has moved to a different row, **101.23 on 1991-03-19**. (Its maximum is now 183.12 on 2025-02-18, a genuine post-2011 high, not a revision.) A further full recalculation was determined appropriate 2025-07-17 **without a named replacement or effective date**. Two consequences: **`assert_causal` cannot detect this** — it guards the construction, not the data, so a green test is false comfort here; and a result is reproducible only against a specific download. *Built:* `scripts/tail_skew.py` writes `results/tail_vintage.json` (source sha256, build date, observed all-time min/max with dates) so the next silent revision is detectable. This is a hole in the repo's reproducibility machinery generally, not a quirk of this signal.
4. **International: definitive no.** Japan — Nikkei publishes the 225 VI (VIX-family), but the free download is a rolling ~3.5yr window and the catalogue contains **no skew index**; JPX daily settlement files carry per-strike IVs sufficient for BKM, but only for the current day (prior dates 404), so only prospective harvesting is possible. Europe — the full VSTOXX family is free back to 1999, but the STOXX Strategy Index Guide contains **zero** occurrences of "skew"; VSTOXX slope and V-VSTOXX are symmetric variance-swap constructions and must never be presented as a skew proxy. Consequence: the shared `run_oos` harness operates on regional *return* panels and **cannot serve this signal at all** — the first signal for which the OOS spine does not apply.
5. **Hygiene, before the look.** A real 7-session hole in the backfill, 2000-09-20…2000-09-29 (dot-com unwind), sits immediately at the restricted start — leave NaN, never interpolate. 49 rows carry `vix` on dates `skew` is absent; spot checks show US market holidays, so reindex to the SKEW trading calendar and drop non-session rows. The put-only variant (SKEW−), the principled fix for the call-side bias, needs paywalled strike-level SPX data — an unfixable limitation, not a v-next task. Deliberately *not* cited: the untraceable "critics found SKEW cannot predict tail risk" claim, and Zhen & Zhang's unpublished working paper — recorded so neither is laundered in later.

## Cross-signal relationships and analysis spec

**Volatility (1.5)** the critical one — both fast, both rise in stress; the distinction is moment × measure; tested at V4 with a kill condition; the two readings are **always shown side by side, never combined**, because "calm, with protection at a decade-high price" only survives if both numbers do. **Funding (7)** genuine one-way link (impaired dealer funding raises the price of supplying protection) — which is the argument *against* folding them: folding a candidate cause into the price it moves destroys the ability to see them disagree, and builds a two-mechanism module (D-07). **Credit/EBP (6)** closest cousin (credit market's price of risk vs the options market's) and the direct precedent for V4's residual remedy. **Absorption (5)** a real confound — BKM (2003) show index densities are far more negatively skewed than single-stock ones *because of systematic risk*, so index skew embeds implied correlation; registered for V7. **Concentration (4)** same composition channel, expected second-order. **Stock-bond (2), valuation (3), crowding (8)** no mechanism links them. The joint reading is deferred to Phase 10.
**Spec (frozen at sign-off):** construction `scripts/tail_skew.py::build` on the shared causal spine, `assert_causal` before the one-look · run against the **frozen sha-stamped vintage**, never a live re-fetch · sample **2000-01-01+** · primary object `S = (100 − SKEW)/10`, native sign · rarity `causal.expanding_percentile(S)` + `causal.expanding_z(S)` · `term_slope` excluded · V4(a) two-term specification fixed above · Level-0 record schema-validated, **no status field** · one look, overnight cooling-off, separate dated sign-off of results.

## What it explicitly does NOT claim (Q6 — the boundary; forbidden vocabulary appears here only to prohibit it)

It reports the price of downside protection relative to at-the-money, its rarity, its direction of travel, and whether it sits at an extreme — and **STOPS**. It is **NOT a crash predictor and NOT a crash-probability estimate**: never a probability, likelihood, odds, or expected frequency of a decline; never "crash risk is high" without the "price of protection against" qualifier; never compared to realized outcomes as accuracy, hit rate, or calibration — the literature's own reading is reverse-causal, that the price rises because the compensation demanded rises. It is not a forecast, not a state or regime label, not a switch or trigger, and emits no {intact | under-test | violated} status. It never states or implies a buy or sell, risk-on/risk-off, an allocation, exposure, weight, tilt, sleeve, cash call, position sizing, risk budget, or any recommendation to obtain or forgo protection — that judgment is the human's, in a separate system this repo does not know exists. It is never combined with the volatility reading into a single number. Martin (2017) — a *bound* on the equity premium from risk-neutral variance under one stated assumption — is the ceiling of what this data supports.
