## Headline conclusions

1. **The engine is broad-market: ERP + yield + real growth + reinvestment, net of dilution.** These are
   identities or paid risks — durable, market-wide, the thing to OWN. This is the literal foundation of the
   "own equity risk efficiently" thesis.
2. **Valuation expansion is NOT a durable source — it is cyclical and rate-driven.** Recent total returns are
   inflated by a spent re-rating tailwind; do not extrapolate them. (Direct discipline for the portfolio test.)
3. **The compounding MATH (variance drain / Kelly / vol-drag) is itself a persistent, portfolio-sizing-level
   force** — this is where "*how* we own the engine" creates value, and it **converges with the defense**
   (the vol-targeting edge, the war-chest deployment). Offense and defense meet here: own broad equity, *size
   and protect and redeploy it well.*
4. **Cross-sectional premia are Tier-2, not the engine.** BAB/low-vol has the best "survives being known"
   case; momentum's mechanism persists but its implementation decays; value is unresolved and decayed;
   quality's crisis-positive payoff undercuts a risk story. All wear the McLean-Pontiff decay tax.
5. **Mechanism→sector test RESULT: no durable mechanism cleanly routes through a sector.** The only candidate
   (intangible-capital scalability) is a **firm-level** mechanism that *currently* concentrates in tech/health
   — but leadership rotates and surplus leaks to consumers. **"Own tech because innovation is durable" is the
   forbidden recent-winner inference.** The defensible expression is broad market + optionally an
   intangible-adjusted quality/value tilt at the firm level.
6. **Bessembinder: ~4% of firms create all net wealth.** Single-name selection is a needle-in-a-haystack
   lottery; broad ownership is how you capture the few winners. This validates avoiding premature single-name
   granularity and argues *for* broad-market + the compounding math.

## Expression-level answer (the granularity question, resolved)

- **Market-wide:** ERP, yield/real-growth/reinvestment, payout/dilution — the durable engine.
- **Portfolio-sizing:** variance-drain / Kelly / vol-drag — the compounding math (where our edge lives).
- **Factor:** BAB, momentum, value, quality — real-ish, decay-prone, secondary.
- **Firm / intangible-intensity:** intangible-adjusted quality/value — the disciplined version of the "tech" instinct.
- **Sector:** NONE durable. **Single-name:** a lottery — own broadly.

## What deserves its own structural investigation (the next decision)

**High value (converges offense+defense):**
- The **compounding / sizing math** — geometric return, vol-drag, the Kelly ceiling, and *when more exposure
  improves compounding vs. just adds risk* — feeding the war-chest deployment and the portfolio prereg.
- The **return-source decomposition** (Grinold-Kroner) on real data — quantify durable engine vs. cyclical
  re-rating, per market, so we don't extrapolate a spent tailwind.

**Candidate Tier-2 exposures (modest, mechanism-gated):**
- **BAB / low-vol** — best durability case.
- **Intangible-adjusted quality/value at the firm level** — the disciplined expression of the growth/tech instinct.

**NOT worth structural investigation (the map already answers them):** sector timing / "own tech"; valuation-
expansion extrapolation; single-name selection; mean-reversion "time diversification."

## Where the profession agrees / disagrees / is genuinely weak

- **Agrees:** ERP is real and paid; the durable engine is yield+growth+reinvestment; CAPM fails; a few
  characteristics carry the cross-section; post-publication decay is real; BAB/value/momentum/quality are the
  "survivor" factors.
- **Disagrees:** the *forward* ERP magnitude (~3.5-4.2% vs. ~5.5% survey vs. realized ~6%); the structural
  explanation of the premium (long-run risk vs. habit vs. rare disasters — observationally equivalent); how
  much of the factor zoo is real (Hou-Xue-Zhang ~65% fail vs. Chen-Zimmermann ~98% replicate); risk vs.
  behavioral for every factor.
- **Weak:** mechanism identification for risk stories (no named bad state; quality pays off *in* crises);
  net-of-cost at-scale survival; mean-reversion once parameter uncertainty enters (Pastor-Stambaugh);
  shareholder capture of innovation; capital-allocation "discipline as alpha."
