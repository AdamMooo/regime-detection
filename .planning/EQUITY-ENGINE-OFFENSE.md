## THE STRICT RULE (Adam) — mechanisms first, exposures second, implementations last

Mirror the defense one-for-one. The defense did NOT start at "should we own bonds?"; it started at "what
risks exist?" The offense must NOT start at factors/QQQ/leverage. **Discover the complete set of candidate
mechanisms FIRST, assuming nothing.** Factors are Tier-2 *hypotheses*, never Tier-1 givens.

| Tier | Defense (done) | Offense (this program) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. MECHANISMS / "why" | what risks exist (drivers → failure archetypes) | **what fundamentally creates long-term equity wealth** |
| 2. EXPOSURES | which sleeves protect against each risk | which exposures capture each durable mechanism (factors appear HERE, as hypotheses) |
| 3. IMPLEMENTATION | ETFs per sleeve | which product captures the exposure most efficiently after fees/tax/turnover/liquidity/capacity/implementation risk |

Defense asks: what risks exist? which exposures protect? which protections are reliable vs conditional?
Offense mirrors: **what creates compounding? which exposures capture it? which are durable vs temporary?**

## The objective (pin it before any data)

> **Maximize long-term geometric wealth creation from owning equity risk while reducing the probability of
> behavioral or capital impairment that prevents compounding.**

Not max Sharpe, not min drawdown, not simply max CAGR, and NOT "the best-performing historical asset/factor/
ETF." Offense and defense are **complements**: the defensive sleeves exist to preserve the ability to own the
equity engine (stay invested, avoid capital impairment, redeploy into it when cheap). Horizon = 20-30+ years.

## Mechanism durability ≠ implementation durability (Adam refinement, 2026-08-02) — the core lens

A mechanism can stay valid while the way we capture it decays. Every candidate is judged on THREE separate
questions, never conflated:

1. **Does the economic mechanism exist?** (is the force real)
2. **Why should it survive after becoming KNOWN?** (a structural friction or a paid risk — not "investors
   don't know winners keep winning")
3. **Does the CURRENT implementation still capture it after costs, crowding, and market adaptation?** (the
   mechanism can persist while a crowded implementation of it does not)

A "yes / yes / no" (real, durable, but no longer capturable net of adaptation) is a *different* verdict from
"no / — / —" (never real) — and the portfolio treats them differently.

## Market-evolution layer (Adam refinement) — persistent forces vs. condition-dependent patterns

Historical evidence is necessary but markets adapt. Every mechanism is classified:

- **Persistent economic/behavioral FORCE** — tied to a constraint likely to remain (productivity, capital
  allocation, leverage aversion, hard-wired behavioral bias + limits to arbitrage). Expected to survive
  changing regimes.
- **Condition-dependent historical PATTERN** — depended on specific conditions and may not recur (valuation
  *expansion* as a return source is cyclical, not permanent; a factor premium may compress after publication;
  the *expression* of growth changes as the economy becomes intangible/tech-driven).

The question is never "what worked in the past?" It is **"which forces are tied to persistent economic or
behavioral constraints likely to remain relevant in the future?"**

## TIER 1 — mechanism discovery (candidate set; no products, no assumed factors)

Candidates that may or may not survive: equity risk premium · earnings growth · capital allocation / buybacks
· dividend reinvestment · valuation mean-reversion · innovation / productivity · cross-sectional mispricing ·
behavioral effects · risk-based premia · (+ anything else the literature supports). For EACH: the 3 durability
questions above + the market-evolution classification + economic reason + OOS/international/publication
survival + disappearance conditions + falsifiers. **Output:** the ranked set of *durable, mechanism-backed
sources* of equity compounding, each tagged persistent-force vs condition-dependent, and mechanism-durable vs
implementation-durable.

## TIER 2 — exposures & their granularity (only after Tier 1)

Which exposures actually capture the surviving mechanisms? Market beta, value, momentum, profitability,
quality, size, sectors, industries, styles, growth, etc. appear here as **hypotheses that must earn their
place** by capturing a genuine Tier-1 mechanism better than alternatives. Falsify: OOS + international +
post-publication decay + spanning + net cost.

**Granularity ladder — go as deep as the MECHANISM expresses, and NO deeper:** market-wide → style/factor →
sector/industry → firm. Two symmetric failure modes: stopping at broad factors when the mechanism actually
expresses through sectors/industries; and dropping to single names too early (noise, survivorship bias, a
less robust framework). The map tells us the natural expression level of each durable source.

**The explicit mechanism→sector test (Adam):** does the top-down process naturally IMPLY certain sectors
today? If innovation/productivity is durable, does it route through e.g. technology — and if so, is the
durable thing the SECTOR, or the underlying mechanism (intangible-capital efficiency) that currently
concentrates there? A sector tilt is justified ONLY when DERIVED from the mechanism, never from "the sector
recently won." "Tech did well → own tech" and "factors worked → use factors" are implementation errors.

**The depth question (governing):** how deep must we go before we can confidently say we understand the
equity engine? Deep enough not to miss a major source of compounding; shallow enough to avoid a data-mining
exercise — each durable mechanism is followed only to the level where it genuinely expresses.

## TIER 3 — implementation (only after Tier 2)

> Which implementation most efficiently captures the desired exposure after costs, taxes, liquidity, capacity,
> turnover, and implementation risk?

Factor ETFs, QQQ, concentrated portfolios, leverage, futures, 2× ETFs, stock selection — implementation
choices for a Tier-2 exposure, never research objectives themselves.

## The offense mathematical toolkit (mirrors the defense — Adam, 2026-08-02)

Math serves ONE purpose: **identify the few economic forces that actually matter — a reducer, not a
complexity-adder.** Each defense tool has an offense twin:

| # | Offense tool | Question it answers | Tier | Defense twin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Return decomposition** (Grinold-Kroner + Campbell-Shiller VAR) | what COMPOUNDS wealth (earnings growth, dividends/buybacks) vs what is CYCLICAL (valuation expansion, inflation)? | T1 | equity-drawdown decomposition, now on RETURNS |
| 2 | **Dimensionality reduction** (PCA/clustering on factor & sector returns) | are many apparent equity drivers the SAME force? do sectors/factors/styles collapse to a few economic dimensions? | T2 | driver PCA (5 macro drivers → ~2 axes) |
| 3 | **Exposure spanning** (regress candidate on a basis of simpler exposures) | is a new exposure genuinely UNIQUE or already captured? e.g. is TECH a unique growth/productivity exposure, or just duration + profitability + momentum? | T2 | sleeve spanning (gold on bonds+commod+trend+cash) |
| 4 | **Robustness & stability** (split-sample · international · regime · pre/post-publication) | does the relationship survive time / countries / regimes / being KNOWN? | all | international replication break-test |
| 5 | **Portfolio-construction math** (geometric return g≈μ−σ²/2, vol drag, Kelly ceiling, concentration vs diversification) | when does MORE exposure improve long-run COMPOUNDING vs just add risk? | T1-Q3 → prereg | drawdown-budget payoff table + war-chest deployment |

Discipline: every tool is **SUBTRACTIVE** — defense PCA collapsed 5 drivers to 2; defense spanning killed
redundant sleeves. The offense tools must likewise REDUCE to the minimal set of durable compounding forces,
never manufacture factors. Tool #3 (the tech spanning test) is the canonical mechanism→sector check: if
"technology" is fully spanned by duration+profitability+momentum, it is NOT a distinct mechanism — it is an
implementation of ones we already own.

## The discipline guardrail

"Be aggressive at the right time" splits: **disciplined** = go aggressive when equity is *cheap* (realized-
drawdown-triggered deployment ladder — mechanical, backward-looking); **the trap** = forecasting that *now* is
the time (time-series equity-engine timing = the closed null → Research Vault, highest bar).

## Discipline

Mechanism gate before any prereg; the literature map + Tier-1 characterization are free (no look); any
POSITIVE claim (a mechanism/exposure *beats* plain beta) needs prereg + overnight cooling-off + dated sign-off.
No selecting on the answer sheet.
